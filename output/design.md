# HW4 Problem 10 - Storefront design

## What changed

- Reworked the hero into a stronger Yale-blue storefront entrance with a large Yale wordmark on the left and a custom vector bulldog mascot on the right.
- Added restrained motion: a drifting hero glow, a gently bobbing bulldog, a moving announcement ticker, and subtle product-image hover movement.
- Strengthened hierarchy with display serif headlines, compact mono labels, larger spacing, and clearer primary calls to action.
- Refined product cards with editorial image framing, stock badges, hover arrows, and clearer price treatment.
- Refined the chat widget with a brighter launch button, stronger panel contrast, and product recommendation cards that feel connected to the store.
- Added responsive layout adjustments so the hero, product grid, and chat remain usable on smaller screens.
- Refined the visual system with a consistent Yale navy, warm cream, coral, and gold palette; clearer type scale; calmer spacing; and stronger active-navigation and focus states.
- Reframed product photos with `object-fit: contain` so catalogue items are no longer cut off, and gave the catalogue, detail, account, and chat views a more consistent retail finish.
- Added reduced-motion support and checked the main routes at desktop and mobile widths.
- Replaced the round bear-like graphic with a broad-muzzled, folded-ear bulldog illustration and reused the face as the chat avatar.
- Enlarged the coral marquee, added a Yale seal/wordmark, and made the lettering shimmer for a clearer brand accent.
- Expanded the chat panel and added an animated speaking mouth plus user-triggered browser voice playback for assistant replies.

## Why it helps customers and the business

The hero immediately communicates Yale identity and gives the storefront a memorable point of view, helping visitors understand the brand before they browse. Motion adds energy and makes the page feel alive without distracting from shopping. Clear hierarchy guides customers from the brand story to the collection, while stock badges, readable prices, and stronger product cards reduce uncertainty. The expressive, voice-enabled chat makes assistance more approachable and gives shoppers who prefer listening another way to use it.
